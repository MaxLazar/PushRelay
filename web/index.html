What it does

PushRelay turns a cheap ESP32 board into a bridge for iPhone notifications. It pairs with your iPhone over Bluetooth the same way a smartwatch does, subscribes to Apple's Notification Center (the ANCS protocol), and forwards every notification — app name, title, body — over WiFi to a delivery service you choose:

  • Bark — free iOS push app
  • Pushover — cross-platform push service
  • MQTT — publish to your own broker for home-automation use

It runs a small web admin UI for configuration, with an app allow-list, a Do Not Disturb schedule, per-app priority, multiple recipients, a custom message format, and a recent-notifications log. After the first USB flash, all further updates happen over WiFi (OTA) — no cable needed again.

🔌
You'll need: an ESP32 board with 8 MB flash (e.g. ESP32-DevKitC / WROVER-E), a USB cable for the first flash, and an iPhone. ANCS is iOS-only in practice — a Mac will pair but never deliver notifications.

1 · Flash the firmware

Nothing to install on your computer. Use desktop Chrome or Edge — Web Serial isn't available in Firefox, Safari, or on mobile.

Web Serial not available — use desktop Chrome or Edge. Serial access was blocked (needs HTTPS). ESP32 · 8 MB flash
  1. Plug the ESP32 into your computer with a USB cable.
  2. Click Connect & install, pick the serial port (CP2102 / USB Serial, or COMx on Windows).
  3. Choose Install PushRelay and confirm the erase prompt. Flashing takes about a minute.

A brand-new, never-flashed ESP32 is fine — the chip's built-in ROM bootloader handles the first flash. If no port appears, install the CP210x USB-UART driver (rarely needed on current Windows/macOS/Linux). Close any other serial monitor (PlatformIO, Arduino IDE) first.

2 · Connect it to WiFi

Right after flashing, still on this page, the installer offers a WiFi setup step over the USB cable: pick your network, enter the password, and when the ESP32 connects the page shows a clickable Visit device link straight to its IP address (http://192.168.x.x). That address is the admin UI — bookmark it.

If the installer doesn't offer WiFi setup

The ESP32 falls back to a captive portal. On your phone or laptop, join the PushRelay-Setup WiFi network; a setup page opens automatically (or browse to 192.168.4.1). Tap Configure WiFi, choose your network, enter the password. The device saves it and reboots onto your network.

🔎
Finding the IP later, without the installer: keep the cable connected, click Logs & Console on this page, press the board's EN/RST button, and watch for WiFi connected: 192.168.…. It's also on the admin UI's Home tab, and the device answers at http://pushrelay.local on most networks.

3 · Pair with your iPhone

  1. On the iPhone, open Settings → Bluetooth.
  2. PushRelay appears under Other Devices. Tap it.
  3. Accept the pairing dialog. PushRelay asks for an authenticated bond, so you may get a passkey confirmation — just accept it (the device auto-confirms its side).
  4. Once paired it stays bonded across reboots, and notifications start flowing to your provider automatically.

4 · Choose a delivery provider

Open the admin UI (the Visit device link, its IP, or http://pushrelay.local), go to the Provider tab, pick Bark, Pushover, or MQTT, paste the credentials, and press Save.

  • Bark: install the Bark iOS app, copy its device key (not a server key), paste it in.
  • Pushover: create an Application for an app token, and use your account user key.
  • MQTT: set broker host, port, topic; optional username/password and TLS. Each notification is published as JSON.

The admin UI

🕒
Do Not Disturb hours are UTC — the device syncs time via NTP with no timezone offset, so convert your local window. The backlog filter stops a reconnect from re-forwarding your whole Notification Center; set the phone timezone offset on the Filters tab to make it exact.

Updating later

No cable needed after the first flash. From the admin UI or your build tools, push new firmware over WiFi (OTA), protected by the OTA_PASSWORD you set at build time. Reflashing does not erase your WiFi credentials, provider keys, or the iPhone bond.

Troubleshooting

iOS Settings never lists PushRelay

Some iOS versions don't show generic BLE accessories under Settings → Bluetooth. Use a free BLE scanner app (e.g. LightBlue) to connect to PushRelay once — that triggers the same system pairing dialog. It's a one-time step; afterwards the device reconnects on its own.

Bonded, but no notifications ever arrive

Almost always a plain ("Just Works") pairing instead of an authenticated one. Forget the device in iOS Bluetooth settings and pair again, accepting the passkey confirmation.

No serial port appears when flashing

Install the CP210x USB-UART driver, try a different USB cable (some are power-only), and close any other serial monitor (PlatformIO, Arduino IDE) that might be holding the port.

Can't find the device's IP address

Try http://pushrelay.local, check your router's client list, or connect over USB and use Logs & Console on this page while pressing the board's EN/RST button to watch for WiFi connected: 192.168.….
